Matt's Owner-Operator Second Brain Guide

An AI chief of staff for your business.

By Matt Shuster · matt@shusteradvisory.com
Based on Andrej Karpathy's LLM wiki pattern.

Much of what you need to run the business is in your head, or somewhere you never get back to: what a customer asked for on a call last month, the price a supplier quoted in the spring, the problem an employee mentioned in passing that you meant to deal with. By Friday it's buried in an inbox, a voicemail or a notebook, or it was never written down at all.

Out of the box, AI starts cold: you re-explain your business, get generic answers, and it does nothing until you ask. Connecting your email helps, but it still pieces each answer together on the spot and keeps no record of what was decided and why.

This is the same AI working as your chief of staff. It's been in every call and email label you connect, and it remembers what was said, what was decided, and why. It briefs you, drafts for you and flags what you'd want to know. Here's what that looks like:

Who's waiting
Your morning briefing lists every quote, callback and promise from last week's calls and email that nobody has closed out, with who's waiting and for how long.
Behind on email
You ask it to draft replies to the email threads it reads. A plain AI reply says “thanks for reaching out.” This one mentions the March quote and the Tuesday job you promised. You copy it into your email, or tell it to save it as a draft.
A price change
A vendor emails a price increase. Next morning's briefing says what changed from what they quoted in March and what it does to your margins.
A pattern
You mention two customers cancelled this month. It points out a third said the same thing on a call in the spring, right after you changed how you schedule, and links both conversations.
Your own thinking
You talk through a new hire with it late Tuesday. By Thursday, your numbers and the option you ruled out are on a page. The note to your partner or your accountant starts from there. You edit and send.
What it is

A memory that keeps itself up to date

Your second brain is a set of pages Claude writes and keeps current about your business, stored in a private folder on GitHub (GitHub calls the folder a repository), plus the instructions and routines that keep it running.

You work with it in Claude Code, the part of Claude that can read and write that folder: at claude.ai/code in a browser, in the Code tab of the Claude app on your phone, or in the Claude desktop app with the session set to Cloud.

Setup

Six steps, the first time on a laptop

Make a free GitHub account, then create one private repository in it.
Account: sign up at github.com/signup and enter the code GitHub emails you. Skip any questions about how you'll use it.
Repository: once you're signed in, create a new repository by opening github.com/new. Name it second-brain, choose Private, tick Add a README file, and click Create repository. Ignore everything else on that screen. You're done when you see a page titled second-brain with a README on it.
Note: If GitHub won't let you sign up or connect from a work computer, it's probably a setting on that computer. Ask whoever manages it.
Go to claude.ai/code and connect it.
Sign in. If it offers a desktop app, click Continue on web at the bottom for now. Approve the GitHub connection. If it offers to install something on your repositories, Skip. Needs a Claude Pro subscription, twenty dollars a month.
Note: If you use the desktop app later, set each new session to Cloud, not Local. A Local session lives only on that computer, and your phone can't pick it up.
Turn off the model improvement setting.
Go to claude.ai/settings/data-privacy-controls and switch it off. It's the difference between your content being kept 30 days and five years. Do it before you add anything.
Pick second-brain from the repository selector, set the model to Sonnet and the mode dropdown to Auto, and paste the prompt below.
It plays back your answers from the form, asks anything you left blank, then builds itself around them. It will ask permission a few times; click Allow.
Connect whatever you want it to read from.
Setup ends by telling you what to connect first, based on your answers.
Email, your drive, your calendar and whatever records your calls: Gmail or Outlook, Google Drive or OneDrive, Fireflies or Otter. Connect what you have today and add more whenever you like.
Then name what's in scope: specific meetings, specific folders, specific email labels and channels. Not the whole inbox. If nobody records your calls, start with email and a weekly report from the software you run jobs or bookings in.
Then point it at your first real material: "read everything in the Pricing folder on my drive." If something lives nowhere it can reach, open your repository on github.com, click Add file then Upload files, and drag it in.
Put the Claude app on your phone and open the Code tab. That's where your second brain lives.
Download it for iPhone or Android and sign in. Regular chats can't see your second brain; the Code tab can, same as claude.ai/code in a browser.
Your routines are already running. Early every morning, a daily update pulls in what's new, updates your pages and rebuilds your briefing. Sunday evenings, a weekly check fixes and tidies the pages. They run on Anthropic's machines, so they happen whether your phone and laptop are on or not. Anything it needs from you shows up at the end of your briefing. Your briefing also shows the date it was last updated. If that isn't today, open your second brain and type: fix my schedules.
You'll see a conversation called "Second brain schedules". It starts your routines. Don't type in it or change its settings. To add or change a routine, just ask in your main conversation: "every Friday at four, rebuild the weekly digest."

What it's for

    
Pages you share instead of explaining

    
Private web pages and dashboards at fixed links, rebuilt on a schedule from what it knows. Ask for one once and it keeps it current. Nobody sees a page until you share it.

    

      

        
Monday morning page

        
What changed with your biggest customers and suppliers last week, what came out of your team meetings, and what's due: quotes waiting on an answer, renewals, bills, licenses. Rebuilt at 6am Monday.

      

      

        
Where new business comes from

        
Send it weekly totals: ad spend, inquiries and sales by source. It tracks what each new customer costs, which sources are growing or slipping, and where people drop off before they buy.

      

      

        
Cash and margins

        
Send it your monthly profit and loss and sales by product or service. It tracks what you make on each, what's slipping, and what to ask your bookkeeper.

      

      

        
Market watch

        
Ask once: "every Monday, check for news on my suppliers, my competitors and rule changes in my line of work." It searches the web and tells you what's new and whether it touches you.

      

      

        
Monthly update

        
For a partner, your team or the bank. The same sections every month, in the same order, in your voice. Teach it the format once and it fills it in.

      

      

        
Weekly digest

        
Everything that changed this week, in ten bullets, on your phone Friday afternoon.

How to ask

    
Getting the most out of it

    
Treat it like a seasoned general manager, not a new hire. You wouldn't walk a good manager through each step. You'd tell them the outcome you need and the lines they can't cross, and let them run it. Do the same here: say what you want and what's off limits, and let it work out the how.

    

      

        
Sales

        
Before: "Make a chart of sales by month."

        
After: "I want to know a slow stretch is coming before the bank balance tells me. You have weekly sales and inquiries. Tell me what you'd watch, and flag it in my briefing when it moves."

      

      

        
Marketing

        
Before: "List where our leads came from this month."

        
After: "I want my marketing money going where the customers who stick around come from. Tell me what we're paying for that isn't paying off."

      

      

        
Your week

        
Before: "Summarize my emails from today."

        
After: "I'm doing too much myself. From my calls and email, tell me which things I keep doing by hand that someone else, or a routine, could take, and start with the one that costs me the most time."

      

    

    
Once a week it'll also suggest one thing worth looking at that you haven't asked about, based on what you've connected and what you've told it matters. Say yes to the ones that matter.

  


  

    
Help from Matt

    
When you want to go further

    
Everything above you can do yourself. Matt also helps with these; ask about any of them.

    

      

        
Set it up for me

        
You'd rather not touch GitHub or settings at all. Matt does the setup with you and shapes the first pages and your morning briefing around how your business actually runs.

      

      

        
One memory for the whole team

        
For the person setting this up for a team, not just themselves. One set of pages everyone works from, people adding their own calls and notes, and a straight answer on what everyone can see.

      

      

        
Get your team using AI well

        
The habits that matter, taught on your people's own work: how to ask for an outcome instead of a task, what to keep out, and which daily chores to hand over first.

      

      

        
Build what it keeps pointing at

        
Some of its suggestions will name things it can't do alone: a dashboard fed by systems that don't talk to each other, an automation that takes steps off someone's plate, a report from a system it can't reach. Matt can build those, and what he builds runs on the same memory, so it starts out knowing your customers and your decisions.
